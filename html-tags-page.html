The Northern Lights: Aurora Borealis

A natural light show created by the Sun and Earth

The Northern Lights, also called the aurora borealis, are glowing waves and curtains of light that appear in the night sky, mainly near Earth's northern polar region. They begin with the solar wind: a stream of electrically charged particles released by the Sun. Earth's magnetic field guides energy and particles toward the upper atmosphere, where energetic particles collide with oxygen and nitrogen. These gases release light, creating the moving patterns people see from the ground and from spacecraft.

Green is one of the most common aurora colors, while red, purple, and blue can also appear. The exact colors depend on which atmospheric gases are involved and the conditions at different altitudes. Dark skies, clear weather, and a location away from bright city lights improve the chance of seeing an aurora. The same kind of phenomenon in the Southern Hemisphere is called the aurora australis, or Southern Lights.
